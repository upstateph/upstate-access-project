What this page measures — and what it doesn't

This page is about travel time to care. For every census tract it computes how long it takes to reach a community health center by walking, driving, and Greenlink bus, routed on the real road network (OSRM) and Greenlink's own published GTFS timetable. Every transit time is door to door: the walk to the stop, the wait, the ride, and the walk at the end.

Read every number as a floor on travel burden, not the typical trip. The model routes each person to whichever site is easiest to reach; many people must instead go where they are established or were referred, and that trip is longer.

Which health center, exactly?

The nearest one on foot or by car, and the best-connected one by bus — not always the nearest, because the bus goes where the bus goes. The model assumes the person can pick; plenty of people can't.

What it does not use

No crash or pedestrian-fatality data goes into any travel time. An earlier version of this site also hosted a statewide pedestrian-safety analysis; that work has been removed and may return as a separate project. An early attempt to connect the two analyses was withdrawn after a null model refuted it.

Known limits, stated plainly

Travel time is an upper bound on real access: the model does not yet know every site's insurance acceptance or opening hours, so a reachable clinic here may still be unusable in practice. Verified operational facts (hours, Medicaid acceptance) are being added one confirmed site at a time and are labeled with how they were confirmed.
