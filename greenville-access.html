What this page measures — and what it doesn't

This page is about travel time to care. For every census tract it computes how long it takes to reach a community health center by walking, driving, and Greenlink bus, using OSRM road-network routing and Greenlink's own published GTFS schedule. Transit is modeled from the real timetable — routes, stops, departure times and waits — not estimated from distance.

Which health center? Whichever is easiest to reach: the nearest one on foot or by car, and the best-connected one by bus — which is not always the nearest, because the bus goes where the bus goes. Either way the model assumes you can pick. Plenty of people can't: they go where they are already established, where their provider is, or where they were referred, and that trip is longer than the one shown here. So read these as a floor on travel burden, not as the typical trip.

It is not built from pedestrian-crash data. An earlier version of this site also hosted a statewide pedestrian-safety analysis; that work has been removed and may return as a separate project. No travel-time number on this page is derived from, weighted by, or adjusted for pedestrian deaths, and an earlier attempt to connect the two analyses was withdrawn after a null model refuted it.

Known limits, stated plainly. Travel time is an upper bound on real access: the model does not yet know which sites accept Medicaid or a given insurance, nor their opening hours, so a reachable clinic here may still be unusable in practice. Both are on the roadmap and both came from physician review.
