Can someone live here without a car?

Type a prospective address. This checks whether a household placed there could reach primary care, the DSS benefits office, workforce services, and a grocery store on foot or by Greenlink bus.

What this answers, and what it will not

The rest of this project asks how far a person is from care. This page asks the same engine a different question, with a different starting point: does this unit work for someone without a car?

It returns four travel times. It does not return a score, a rating, or a recommendation. A single number would hide which of the four failed, and that is the only part anyone can act on. Whether a unit is right for a household is a judgment for the person who knows the household.

Reachable means: within a 20-minute walk, or a Greenlink trip with at most one transfer and at most a 30-minute wait, taken as the median over departures across the weekday-midday hour. Modeled from public data, not observed trips.

Greenville County only. Greer, Piedmont and Fountain Inn straddle county lines, so a real local address can fall outside the modeled area; you will be told which county it landed in rather than that it failed.

Nothing typed here is stored. The address is used to compute the answer and then discarded. No accounts, no logging.

Across the whole county

Every one of Greenville County's 123 census tracts, scored the same way:

32.5%of tracts reach all four without a car
22.8%of residents live in those tracts
49.6%of tracts reach none of the four

There is almost no middle ground. 61 tracts reach zero of the four and 40 reach all four. Only 22 sit anywhere in between. A unit is usually either connected or stranded, which is what makes checking one worthwhile before a lease is signed rather than after.

The DSS row is not a rounding artifact. One DSS office serves the entire county, at 352 Halton Rd. For the third of tracts that can reach it at all, the median trip is an hour and forty minutes each way. Enrolling in SNAP or Medicaid is a whole day.

The part that matters for placement

Tracts that reach all four have a median household income of $66,886 and 7.6% of households without a vehicle. Tracts that reach none have a median income of $85,869 and 3.5% without a vehicle. The places with no car-free access are the places where nearly everyone drives.

Which is exactly why a neighborhood average cannot answer this. A household leaving homelessness is unlikely to have a car regardless of what its new neighbors own. Placing one into a tract where 96% of households drive puts them somewhere the transit answer was never designed for. The average describes the neighborhood; only the unit describes the household.
